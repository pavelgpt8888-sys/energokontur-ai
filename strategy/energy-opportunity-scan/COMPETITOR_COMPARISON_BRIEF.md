# Comparable competitor brief — Industrial RFQ/quote workflow

Purpose: obtain *comparable* public/voluntary vendor input from Belarus-serving suppliers; not a deceptive fictitious customer tender. Introduce yourself accurately as an automation company undertaking vendor/partner landscape research. Do not claim a real customer, real budget, signed mandate or permission that does not exist.

## Reference process (fully SYNTHETIC; quote-discovery only)

A mid-size electrical equipment distributor/manufacturer has:
- 150–200 incoming technical RFQs per month via email/form/messenger, some with 10–80 page PDFs;
- 3 commercial + 2 engineering people;
- 1C accounting/inventory, an existing CRM, 10–20 suppliers with different price formats;
- manual data entry, engineering selection, BOM verification, quote preparation and discount approval;
- variable availability/prices; payment follow-up and quotation status tracked by staff.

**No access to production data or personal information is requested for the initial discussion.**

## Three scopes to quote separately

**S1 — Narrow feature.** PDF/email → structured RFQ → human-reviewed draft specification/CP. No claim of full process automation.

**S2 — Quote-to-cash pilot.** S1 + approved vendor prices and stock → margin/discount gate → quote → CRM next action → ERP order state → invoice/payment exception queue (read-only bank + human approvals).

**S3 — Managed service.** S2 + monthly operation/SLA, human escalation queue, monitoring, updates and a measurable monthly process report.

## Questions to each provider
1. Which of S1–S3 would you deliver yourself and which via a partner? Where are the boundaries?
2. What data access, permissions and information-security prerequisites are required?
3. Estimated one-off implementation fee by scope; ongoing fees; license/API/infrastructure not included.
4. Expected calendar duration and person-hours; major dependencies.
5. Do you have a similar **verified client** willing to provide a reference, or only a demonstration?
6. What happens when BOM matches ambiguously, current price is unknown, or margin gate is violated?
7. Who accepts engineering and financial decisions? Are there audit logs and rollback paths?
8. Which existing systems can remain as sources of truth, and what API/connector is required?
9. Who owns source code, configuration, training/curation artefacts and usage rights?
10. SLA: hours of support, issue response, ongoing evaluation, version updates, change request billing.

## Comparable recording fields
Date, provider, respondents, observed portfolio proof, scope S1/S2/S3, exclusions, setup BYN, recurring BYN, pass-through costs, timeline, review approvals, data/on-prem, references, support/SLA, contractual restrictions, unknowns.

## Interpretation
Do not compare S1 "from 2,000 BYN" with S2 cross-system 30,000 BYN pilot or S3 managed annual contract as if the same product. Record exact scope and customer value. Do not infer market TAM or company revenue from posted prices.

## Market validation alongside competitor calls
Conduct direct owner/COO/sales director interviews with real firms to learn last actual lost or delayed quote, its margin and volume; confirm whether customer actually wants **delivery of result** rather than a software license.
