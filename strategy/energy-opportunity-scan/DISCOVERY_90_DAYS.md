# 90-day discovery experiment: Energy → Industrial Operations

Date: 2026-09-29. Owner role: founder as commercial/industrial process architect. No customer names, contact data or fabricated interviews included.

## Question to decide
Which wedge gives the best combination of:
1. paid customer problem with visible economic owner;
2. contract value allowing delegated delivery;
3. repeatable product kernel;
4. short enough first sales;
5. high founder fit and legal/data feasibility?

## Weeks 1–2: remote pre-audit and 18 interviews

**No costly code first.** Build 4 synthetic companies for dry-runs:
- plant with 2 departments, manual RFQ/procurement, downtime and energy load;
- electrical equipment distributor with orders and one 1C accounting instance;
- EPC contractor with tender, scope changes, PTO and project profitability;
- energy/service operator with repeated inspection and documentation.

Clearly label all simulated metrics SYNTHETIC; never report them as actual enterprise facts. For real prospect research, follow Automation Architect FACT/CLIENT_CLAIM/HYPOTHESIS/QUESTION rules.

Interview sample:
- 3 industrial manufacturers;
- 3 B2B equipment wholesalers/distributors;
- 3 EPC/building/installation contractors;
- 3 industrial service/maintenance companies;
- 3 logistics/warehouse operators;
- 3 energy/utility-adjacent or energy-intensive enterprises.

**Every interview should reveal** the most expensive real recent process failure, average volume, baseline time or leakage, data sources, owner, actual signoff path, and a quote-test for audit/pilot willingness to pay.

Ask for one anonymized real artifact (RFQ, invoice, exception, closeout checklist etc.) with authorized usage before using it in prototype. If consent/legal basis or storage restrictions are unknown, use synthetic input.

## Weeks 3–4: offer tests, not another broad idea scan

Put forward exactly three scoped offers:

1. **Industrial Revenue Desk:** RFQ → technical quote → approved offer → CRM next action (C46/C47/C48). First measurable result: quote SLA and omissions.
2. **Project Margin & Evidence:** field/RFI changes → proof → variation request → project budget view (C43/C44/C45/C55/C56). First measurable result: unbilled change detection and shorter document closeout.
3. **Industrial Reliability & Compliance:** equipment/energy/process records → due dates/validated anomalies → engineer-approved action (C31/C32/C33/C34/C59). First metric: overdue critical tasks and time-to-verified-information.

The buyer must choose a bounded process. An audit at 8–18k BYN or a pilot at 12–50k BYN is only a TEST range dependent on the exact scope; never send a fixed quote from this template without discovery.

## Weeks 5–8: paid work and reuse measurement

Targets (internal experiment, not forecast):
- 2 paid audits across at least 2 independent companies;
- 1 paid pilot with a concrete baseline;
- log reusable components vs custom integration time;
- weekly founder time ledger (selling, discovery, solution acceptance, coding, administration);
- proof of result verified by customer owner.

No automatic payments, switching commands, document publication or other safety-critical AI actions. Human review and traceability first.

## Weeks 9–12: decide business model

| Observed pattern | What it implies |
| --- | --- |
| Same process purchased ≥3 times; ≥70% logic reusable | Convert service into module/vertical SaaS candidate |
| Customers keep saying «делайте за нас» and pay for operations | Managed service / SLA |
| Enterprise design partners with approved data + budget | Fund Energy Knowledge AI BY / Energy Pack |
| One-off work requires >60% bespoke engineering | Stay service-led or narrow ICP |
| Low WTP, no measurable result, long sales, no budget owner | Kill/reframe |

## 5-field opportunity card (one per buyer problem)
1. **Buyer/ICP:** not «energy companies», but roles and company scale.
2. **Value leakage:** concrete money or time with baseline and period.
3. **Existing substitute:** staff/1C/CRM/Excel/vendor/regulatory system.
4. **First offer economics:** audit price, delivery hours, pilot scope, monthly support and compliance.
5. **Go/no-go evidence:** paid intent, realistic integration, repeatability across 3 buyers.

## Example of non-deceptive ROI
A distributor reports (CLIENT_CLAIM) 150 RFQs per month and 60 minutes of potentially removable handling each. At an assumed loaded 30 BYN/h this is 4,500 BYN of **capacity released** monthly, NOT cash savings automatically. If the proposed ongoing service costs 2,000 BYN/month and implementation 20,000 BYN, that labour-time example alone does not prove a favourable cash ROI. Need verify whether the capacity cuts overtime/contractor spend or leads to additional *profitable* orders. Measure response time, accepted quotes, contribution margin and causality against a baseline.

## Founder operating model
- >=50% of founder hours: discovery, B2B sales, partnerships and customer success;
- 20–30%: solution framing, QA/business outcome acceptance;
- <=15–20%: personal coding; delegate with reviewed PRs/tests;
- remainder: product strategy and accounting/admin.

Quarterly key result is **paid validated process improvement**, not 20 demos or another 500 ideas. Keep Energy Knowledge AI draft PR #3 isolated until it has paid design partners.

## Decision checkpoint
At day 90, compare: signed revenue, cash contribution, real hours founder spent, support load, verified customer benefit, average deal cycle, and reusable code %. A niche with lower gross ticket but fast sales/high retention can outperform a bank or utility enterprise proposal that never closes.
