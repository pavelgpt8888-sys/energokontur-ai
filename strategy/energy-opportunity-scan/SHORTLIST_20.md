### Group into offers, not 20 unrelated builds

**Offer A — Industrial Revenue Desk:** C46 + C47 + C48. Repeatable customer promise: fewer lost RFQs, faster source-backed quotes, visible sales pipeline.

**Offer B — Industrial Procurement Control Tower:** C49 + C50 + optional C57. Promise: faster purchasing, controlled prices and lower inventory friction.

**Offer C — Project Margin & Evidence:** C43 + C44 + C45 + C55 + C56. Promise: from field facts to payable/approved scope and margin control. Human retains engineering and contract approval.

**Offer D — Reliability & Compliance:** C31 + C32 + C33 + C34 + C59. Promise: measurable operational data and verified regulatory workflow (no autonomous switching).

**Offer E — AI-native Shared Back Office:** C57 + C71 + C75. Promise: provide a managed process function to several customers, reuse common modules, customer still owns key approvals.

### Commercial gates before productization
- 10–15 interviews across at least three verticals.
- At least 3 independent buyers describe the same expensive workflow in their own language.
- At least 2 sign a paid discovery or pilot, not just praise a demo.
- Baseline and measurable acceptance criteria exist.
- >=70% implementation logic reusable by the third customer, after separating universal kernel, domain pack and customer adapter.
- 1-month postlaunch support cost measured before promising a SaaS margin.
- Payment, switching, grid operations, safety, normative publication and other critical actions require explicit approval.

### Deliberately outside first-quarter sales
C13–C15 GETs, C16 transformer health, C25–C27 grid-scale planning, C61–C63 OT security, C67–C69 DC engineering. These may support larger enterprise contracts, but need pilots, data rights, specialist engineering and longer sales. Keep as strategic options; do not confuse a global technology trend with Belarus-market willingness to pay.
