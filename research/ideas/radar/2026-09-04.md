## 1. Virtualized protection and control (vPAC)

Instead of one physical relay/IED per protection/control function, multiple protection applications run as software on redundant IEC 61850-3-grade industrial servers. Siemens SIPROTEC V states that one server-based platform can consolidate functions of up to 60 SIPROTEC 5 devices. Siemens publishes project-derived KPIs of up to 25% CAPEX reduction, 45% smaller protection/control building footprint, 80% copper-cable cost reduction, up to 20% lifecycle savings, and up to six months faster execution. RTE's VIP'R project plans 63 kV lab deployment in 2026 with ABB and Schneider virtual protections and field parallel operation in 2027.

Commercial logic: this is the deeper layer behind the “digital substation” concept — not merely process bus or standard drawings, but removal of dedicated relay hardware. Potential BY/RU product is an open, vendor-neutral local vPAC platform on certified rugged servers, with selected protection vendors as software workloads.

Key risk: deterministic real-time behavior and fail-safe architecture are safety-critical. RTE cites 250 μs sampled-data processing and breaker action inside 30 ms with >99.9% availability targets. EAEU deployment would require full protection-function validation, IEC 61850 interoperability, cybersecurity and utility acceptance; conventional EAC certification alone is not enough.

Sources: Siemens SIPROTEC V; LF Energy SEAPATH / RTE VIP'R; CIGRE 2026 vendor ecosystem.

## 2. Supercritical CO2 waste-heat power generation

China's “Super Carbon No.1 / Chaotan One” at Shougang Shuicheng Steel is the first commercial-scale industrial sCO2 waste-heat power project: 2×15 MW. Unit 1 entered commercial operation in Dec 2025; unit 2 connected May 2026. Public operating data report >32.856 million kWh from one unit during roughly six months, all consumed on site, with multi-month stable operation.

How it works: waste heat drives a closed Brayton-cycle loop using CO2 above its critical point (~31°C / 7.38 MPa), avoiding steam evaporation/condensation and allowing compact turbomachinery and higher efficiency at small/medium heat-source temperatures.

Commercial target: steel, ferroalloys, cement, glass, chemicals and other plants with large continuous waste-heat flows. Russia is more attractive than Belarus due industrial scale. Main due-diligence question is installed $/kW and LCOE versus ORC and conventional steam waste-heat recovery.

Status: RESEARCH / LOCALIZE. Equipment localization could eventually focus on recuperators, heat exchangers, seals, compressors/turbines, control and balance-of-plant rather than copying the whole cycle.

## 3. Graphene-copper conductor/contact materials

CHINT publicly reports graphene-copper conductors up to ~108% IACS and graphene-enhanced high-conductivity copper contact material as a potential drop-in substitute for silver-based contacts. Its white paper also claims graphene composite surface treatment can raise silver-plating wear resistance by >10×. Independent 2026 research supports the underlying effect, although published results vary strongly: Wuhan University of Technology reported 102.74% IACS with 270 MPa tensile strength after annealing; a 2026 Small paper found graphene-enhanced conductivity depends strongly on graphene continuity and copper geometry and can reach large gains under optimized conditions. New Chinese patents in 2026 address scalable interface engineering and composite-wire manufacture.

Commercial logic: even a 2–5% validated conductivity gain in high-current busbars, transformer windings, contacts or high-temperature conductors can create material savings or lower I2R losses. Contacts are especially attractive because they are small, high-value and can replace silver-containing parts.

Risk: vendor claims of 108–109.6% IACS need independent certification on production-scale material, not laboratory coupons. Priority is to obtain real samples, price/kg, batch consistency, joining/plating behavior and accelerated aging data.

Status: RESEARCH / LOCALIZE.

## 4. Industrial high-temperature heat pumps, 120–180°C

Commercial product range is expanding rapidly. Copeland/Vilter in 2026 markets 400 kW–5 MW industrial heat pumps delivering process heat up to 180°C. Chinese suppliers already advertise 120°C steam heat pumps; one Chinese vendor states ~50% lower energy use versus gas-boiler heat supply when suitable waste heat is available and lists CE/TUV/CB plus EAC among certifications, which must be verified per exact model. IEA 2026 says heat pumps below 120°C are already commercial and systems below ~160°C are starting commercial deployment.

Commercial logic for BY/RU is strong: food, dairy, breweries, paper, wood, textiles, chemicals, laundries, district heating and plants with simultaneous cooling/waste-heat and steam/hot-water demand. Sell on measured heat balance and COP, not “green” marketing.

Status: IMPORT NOW / OEM CANDIDATE. This is one of the fastest routes to revenue because China already has manufacturable hardware and the sales case can be built around avoided gas/steam cost.

## 5. 13.8 MW integrated PCS substation in one 40-ft container

At CIGRE 2026 CHINT presented a 40-ft integrated BESS substation combining ~13.8 MW PCS, transformer and clean-air MV switchgear, with claimed up-to-99% system efficiency and <30 ms full charge/discharge transition. This moves integration from “PCS + transformer + MV skid + site engineering” to a factory-built module.

Commercial value: less civil work, less site wiring, faster commissioning, standardized QA and higher MW per transportable module. Best route for BY/RU would be import/OEM of the integrated package rather than local PCS design initially.

Risk: current evidence is exhibition/product material, not enough independent field-deployment data. Require type-test reports, thermal derating curves, fault-current behavior, serviceability, transport constraints and full price per MW before promotion.

Status: OEM CANDIDATE / RESEARCH.

## 6. Closed-loop geothermal with supercritical CO2

China Huaneng's 2,500 m Zhengzhou demonstration circulates supercritical CO2 instead of groundwater. Reported figures: ~20% higher heat-extraction capacity and ~10% lower pumping energy than water-based operation. The closed-loop design avoids groundwater extraction and formation contamination.

Commercial logic: potentially useful where geothermal rock heat exists but water resource/permeability is poor. More relevant to Russia than Belarus. Current project supplies heating-scale output rather than utility-scale electricity.

Status: RESEARCH. Main unknowns are drilling CAPEX, CO2 containment/leakage, long-term well integrity and heat decline.

## 7. Chinese transformer export / fast-delivery opportunity

Jiangsu Huapeng produced 15 × 35 kV / 6 MVA pad-mounted transformers for European data-center projects and obtained BSI carbon-neutral certification for the batch. The technical novelty is limited; the commercial signal is strong: Chinese transformer vendors are using delivery speed, capacity and international certification to enter markets suffering transformer shortages.

Opportunity for BY/RU: source qualified Chinese power/distribution transformers where domestic lead time or price is problematic, then localize engineering, acceptance testing, service, spares and possibly enclosures/buswork. This is an IMPORT NOW opportunity, not a frontier R&D bet.

Key due diligence: real factory load, short-circuit test certificates, KEMA/third-party history, losses under local standards, warranty enforcement, sanctions/payment/logistics and EAEU conformity.

## 8. Automated offshore vessel charging cable control

Stillstrom/Maersk demonstrated automated cable deployment and active cable-length management for charging vessels held on dynamic positioning near offshore wind infrastructure. DNV observed the qualification trial; next steps are full integrated vessel and sea trials.

The technology solves a real electromechanical barrier: a charging connector suspended ~15.5 m above a moving vessel must track relative vessel motion without damaging the cable or connector.

Commercial relevance: low for Belarus, niche but potentially meaningful for Russian offshore oil/gas, ports and future offshore wind. More interesting as a cable-handling mechanism / connector product than as an energy platform.

Status: WATCH / RU.
